# threeditor projection mapping

[Back to threeditor](../README.md) · [Editor reference](editor-reference.md)

## Projection mapping

1. Add a **Projector** from **Add → Light**. Move and rotate the rig to aim it along local **−Z**. The wireframe shows the rectangular projection.
2. Under **Object → Projection**, use **Choose media…** to select an image or video. You can also drag media from **Assets** onto the projector’s outliner row. A new projector starts with a calibration grid.
3. In the projector’s **Script** parameters, set throw ratio (distance divided by image width), aspect ratio, intensity, range, falloff and shadows. Aspect ratio `0` follows the source. Enable **Calibration grid** to check alignment with media already assigned.

Select a projector and click **Object → Set as main camera** to use it for Play, presentation, and exports. **Projector output → Rendered lens view** renders the scene from the projector’s position and optics. **Masked texture** sends the fitted source image, video, or animation with its mask applied. Both modes preserve the lens aspect ratio.

Use **Open texture output** on any projector to send its masked texture to a separate live window while you edit. Move the window to the physical projector’s display and double-click the image for fullscreen. The same masked texture continues to light the virtual scene. For overlapping projectors, set the four **Edge blend** widths and **Edge blend gamma** in the Projector component. Widths are percentages of the lens frame. The fades apply after warp and masks, so virtual lighting and physical texture outputs use the same picture.

Use a lit mesh material such as MeshStandardMaterial for the projection surface. With renderer shadows enabled, turn on **Cast shadow** for blockers and **Receive shadow** for projection surfaces. The projector illuminates lit meshes. The Projector component also exposes **Shadow map resolution**, **Shadow depth bias (millionths)**, **Shadow normal bias**, and **Shadow filter radius**. The filter radius follows the shadow filter selected in Project renderer settings. Point clouds and Gaussian splats can serve as spatial references.

Named browser saves and playable exports retain projector settings and source media. **Editable HTML** also retains source files for continued editing.

### Look through a light

Select a projector or light in the viewport’s **View** selector, or select it in the outliner and click **Look through** in **Object**. Projectors and spotlights use their beam framing. Directional lights use the shadow camera’s orthographic bounds, and point, hemisphere, and area lights show a view from the light’s position.

Choose **Split View → Vertical Split** to keep the **Director** camera on the left and the selected view on the right. Navigate in the left pane. The light pane follows the light’s position, aim, and optics. Choose **Solid** to see the director’s guides and the orange camera and projector bodies. Each lens marks the start of its frustum. Play and exports use the assigned main camera. The split and light panes use the direct renderer while you work.

Select an unlocked projector or spotlight and choose **Object → Edit mask in view**. When looking through the light, use the **Edit light mask** icon in Compact mode or **Edit mask** beside the viewport tool menu in other styles. Draw over the receiver in the light pane, drag the handles, choose **Light inside** or **Block inside**, then apply. In a split view you can check the result from the director at the same time.

### Multi-shape mask editor

Select a projector or spotlight and choose **Editor Type → Mask mode** in the app’s top bar or **Modeling → Open 2D mask editor**. The mask workspace takes the place of the 3D viewport while the Outliner and properties remain available. Open the same menu and choose **3D View** to return. The **Edit light mask** viewport tool still supports drawing directly through the light camera. Draw separate polygons, freehand outlines, rectangles, or ellipses. Double-click the first point or press **Enter** to close a polygon. Drag a vertex to reshape the contour, drag inside a shape to move it, or double-click an edge to insert a point. Use the Push and Smooth brushes to reshape the selected contour. The shape list lets you rename, duplicate, or delete individual shapes.

**Light inside** shapes combine into one aperture. **Block inside** shapes cut holes out of that aperture. A light with only cutouts starts with its full frame. Click **Apply masks** to save the set in one undoable change.

Use **Modeling → Pick scene outline** to search for existing closed Shape and Path objects. **Fit outline to frame** scales a copy into the mask frame. **Project outline in place** uses the current light camera and the outline's scene transform. Both add a shape to the current set.

### Image and vector editing

Choose **Editor Type → Image and vector mode** to draw colored shapes and cutouts with the same tools as the mask editor. Use **Draw stroke** for open freehand lines. In point editing, press **F** to open or close the selected contour. **Create scene curve** copies the selected contour into the XY plane as an editable path. **Create filled scene shape** copies a closed fill as an editable surface. The drawing frame spans two scene units and its center maps to the origin. Use **Copy from scene in place** to bring scene paths and shapes into that frame at their world XY coordinates, or **Fit outline to frame** to center and scale a copy.

**Apply drawing** saves the contours with the project. **Open reference image** loads a local image for tracing or compositing during the editing session. Hold Shift during a Push brush stroke to smooth the contour.

**Export SVG** saves the vector shapes. **Export PNG** renders a 1024 × 1024 image with the reference included when visible. Toggle **Show reference image** to export the drawing on a transparent background.

### Target silhouettes, warp, and UVs

- Select a projector and choose a receiver mesh under **Modeling → Auto-mask target**. The mask follows the silhouette as you move the projector, adjust the lens, or edit the target. A hand-drawn polygon can further trim the result. The mask also applies to **Masked texture** output.
- With an auto-mask target assigned, choose **Modeling → Check projector coverage** after moving the rig or its receiver. Green shows clear receiver samples and orange shows samples blocked by opaque meshes. Click a blocker in the report to select and frame it. Turn on **Projector coverage overlay** at the bottom of the projector pane to see the map over the lens view while you edit in the Director pane. The overlay resamples after scene edits and lens changes settle. Use **Refresh projector coverage** for a denser check, including changes made directly by scripts. The check uses authored mesh visibility and lens range before masks and warp. It samples opaque meshes and skips transparent materials, point clouds, and splats.
- Choose **Modeling → Edit projection warp** to adjust the 4 × 4 sampling grid. Drag a handle to choose which source pixel lands at that position in the projector frame, then click **Apply**. Use the calibration grid and look through the projector to check correction on the receiver. **Clear warp** restores the original mapping.
- To map an image onto a mesh material, select the mesh and choose **Editor Type → UV mode** or **Modeling → Edit UVs**. Choose **Unwrap from seams** for hand-cut islands, **Smart unwrap** for automatic chart cuts, **Lightmap pack** for baking, or use a cube, cylinder, sphere, or planar projection. Project from View uses the 3D pane camera. Projectors use their lens and frame. Select **Auto layout**, drag points to refine the mapping, and click **Apply**. Shift-click adds points to the selection. If the material has an image map, it appears behind the UV wireframe.

Try these controls in **DDA Gallery · Lofts and cinema fragments**. Each projector already has a target assigned. To check the physical feed, make a projector the main camera and select **Masked texture** before starting Play. [Gallery walkthrough and sources](../editor/examples/gallery/README.md)

- New cubes start with a connected UV cross. For an existing rectangular box, choose **Connected cube net** and **Auto layout** to unfold the six faces along five shared edges. **Cube projection** still makes six separate islands. Authored quads display their boundaries in the UV editor.

- In **UV Editing**, use **Mark or clear UV seam** and click an edge in the UV view. You can also select edges in the 3D pane in Edit Mode and use **Mark selected 3D edges as seams** or **Clear seams on selected 3D edges**. Marked seams appear pink. Choose **Unwrap from seams → Auto layout** to flatten the connected surface across uncut edges, including across material boundaries. For example, one lengthwise seam opens a tube wall into a single UV island. If a chart cannot flatten within the overlap and stretch limits, add seams or use **Smart unwrap**. **Smart unwrap** and **Lightmap pack** honor marked seams and can add their own cuts. **Apply** saves the layout and seam marks with the mesh.

- Choose **Pack existing islands → Auto layout** to fit the current UV islands into the texture square after editing. Set the margin in pixels at 1024 × 1024. Packing preserves orientation, shape, and relative scale and applies one common scale across the atlas. Unpin vertices before packing, and choose **Apply** to commit.

- In **UV Editing → Texture Bake**, choose **Light and shadow guide**, **Scene appearance**, **Masked projector frame**, or **Pencil and surface marks**. Pick the 3D view, a scene camera, or suitable light or projector, and bake at 256, 512, or 1024 pixels. The marks pass captures Pencil ribbons, surface paths, and geometry paint with their transparency. Occluded and unpainted texels in that pass stay transparent. Preview the result in the UV editor, download the PNG, save it to **Assets → Project**, or add an undoable unlit guide mesh. Use packed UVs for unique surface coverage. The bake is a snapshot of what the source view sees. Use WebGL and apply any mesh modifiers first.

### Measure and draw on receivers

**Pencil** and **Measure** sit near the top of the left shelf in every viewport mode. The Pencil glyph or **D** activates freehand drawing. The shared **Placement** menu sets where the stroke starts. **Mesh Surface** follows visible mesh or scan surfaces and falls back to the ground where the pointer misses. **Ground Plane** draws at world Y = 0. **View Plane** goes through the 3D Cursor, and **Surface Perpendicular** starts on the surface and draws out from it.

Choose **Pencil drawing plane** for the body of the mark. **Follow placement** uses the shared placement throughout the stroke. With **Surface perpendicular**, a stroke that starts in empty space stands upright through the pointer’s ground-plane intersection. **View at start**, **Upright from start**, **Top from start**, **Front from start**, and **Side from start** fix a plane through the actual first point. **Follow stroke depth** uses the depth of nearby strokes as you draw and keeps the last sampled depth when you move away. To connect marks, open the chain glyph next to the brush swatch and choose **Any segment**, **Stroke starts**, or **Stroke ends**. In **Properties → Tool**, **Pencil attach points** determines whether the first point, both ends, or all points of the new stroke attach. A line and ring preview the connection as you approach a stroke. Hold **Cmd** or **Ctrl** at either endpoint to bypass attachment there. The **Pencil plane guide** shows the orientation and the **Pencil height guide** drops a dashed line to the ground footprint. Press **B** to lock the surface or stroke under the pointer, or the ground-plane intersection in empty space. A diamond marks the reference point and the chosen drawing plane passes through it. A locked path becomes the attachment target. Press **B** or **Esc** to release the lock.

Hold **Shift** while drawing for a temporary lazy pen or enable **Stabilize strokes** for every gesture. **Stabilizer radius**, **Stroke smoothing**, and **Simplify tolerance** are in **Properties → Tool**. Cleanup preserves attached points on planar strokes. Circular, radial, parallel, grid-axis, and isometric guides are also available. End near the start to close a single contour, then use its right-click actions to make a shape or mesh, or reuse the outline in a mask.

The Pencil toolbox sits in one row above the timeline. Use **Placement** in the viewport toolbar for the starting surface or plane. The default ink is light blue. Pencil has its own **Draw editable stroke**, **Draw Pencil splats**, **Paint mesh texture**, **Erase mesh texture**, and **Erase pencil strokes** tools. The stroke eraser removes the editable strokes touched by the gesture and undoes them together. Texture tools use the visible UV-mapped surface and share a pressure-sensitive brush palette with the 2D editors. Choose a textured tip such as Charcoal, Chalk, Ink Rough, or Dry Brush. Use **Brush grain**, **Grain scale**, and **Brush grain pattern** to adjust coverage and texture. For brush marks, **Brush spacing** sets dab distance as a fraction of size, while **Brush tip aspect** and **Brush tip angle** shape the nib. Pen pressure can drive size and opacity independently in the 3D Pencil and the raster paint tools in the viewport, UV, and Image & Vector editors. Size responds to pressure by default. The editable 2D contour Pencil uses its set width and opacity. Use **Save brush preset** to keep a named copy of the current settings. **Saved brush presets** recalls it in any of the shared palettes. Saving under an existing custom name updates it. **Restore saved brush preset** reverts adjustments to that preset. Presets are stored in the browser and can be reused across projects. The Pencil row keeps the brush swatch, color, size, strength, drawing plane, and attachment controls. The full settings are in **Properties → Tool**. From the viewport **N** sidebar, use **Open active tool properties** to open the same tab. Measure calibration and binding, Surface Paint appearance and placement, and Sculpt brush settings also appear in the Tool tab when active. New 3D strokes render with rounded brush geometry and keep their editable control points. In Object Mode, click a Pencil stroke or cross its visible path with Box Select to select it. **Cmd-drag** on a number to scrub it, use **Shift-drag** for fine adjustment, or **Ctrl-drag** to snap. **[** and **]** change the brush size, and **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** adjust strength.

**Draw Pencil splats** paints Gaussian samples using the Pencil’s color, pressure, tip shape, and grain. Use the shared Placement menu and drawing guides for the stroke. Mesh Surface also lets you paint on a visible point cloud or scan. The samples are saved in **Pencil splats** batches that follow the receiver. Undo removes one gesture at a time.

Select a **Pencil stroke** and press **Tab** to edit its control points. With the Select tool, drag a point directly, Shift-click to toggle individual points, or use the region selection tools and **G**, **R**, or **S**. Insert and delete points from the modeling actions. Use **Sculpt Mode** or the Sculpt brush glyph to reshape the stroke. **Stroke thickness** and **Stroke opacity** are available in **Properties → Tool** for Pencil strokes. Hold **Ctrl** to reduce either value as you brush, or **Shift** to temporarily smooth the stroke.

Enter **Sculpt Mode** directly from Object Mode. The brush affects eligible selected objects and editable objects in selected groups. With no object selected, the brush can work across visible, unlocked editable meshes, paths, and shapes. A gesture can edit several objects in one undo step. Press **Esc** during the gesture to cancel it.

In **UV Editing**, the left shelf has point and region selection, Move, Rotate, Scale, a 2D cursor pivot, and Pencil and Erase Texture on the selected material slot. Use **Select UV islands** to click or drag an entire island. **L** adds the island under the pointer, **Shift+L** removes it, and **A** selects all. Use **G**, **R**, or **S** to transform the selection. Use **Edit UV vertices** to drag individual UV vertices. **Push**, **Smooth**, and **Pinch** reshape vertices with a brush. An active selection limits the brush to those vertices. Hold **Shift** to smooth temporarily. **[** and **]** adjust radius, while **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** adjust strength. Press **P** to pin selected vertices against sculpting or **Alt+P** to unpin them. Pinned vertices have a pink ring and can still be moved with the transform tools. Enable **Protect UV island borders** in the sculpt settings to brush only the interior. Unpin before replacing the layout. Use **Apply** to save UV coordinates and pins. A texture stroke commits directly to its material and has one project undo step. Use **Sample Brush Color** in UV Editing to pick from the selected material’s image, or in Image & Vector to pick from the visible drawing and reference image. Both tools update the shared Pencil color and return to the paint tool.

In **Image & Vector**, **New texture** makes a transparent canvas at the requested size. Open a local still image or choose an existing texture from **Project Assets**. **Pencil** draws editable contours and taps make dots. **Brush** and **Erase Brush Marks** use the shared raster brush. The **Contours and reference** panel contains contour tools and an **Annotation** toggle for pencil marks. Scene pencil marks have the same toggle in **Object Properties**. Annotations are visible in editing views and omitted from Preview, renders, and texture exports.

Use **Apply drawing** to keep editable strokes and source references in the project. **Save texture to Project assets** creates a PNG at the canvas dimensions without overwriting its source. Select the result under **Assets → Project** to assign it to a material or projector. Use **Choose reference from assets** or **Open reference image** to add a guide. Drag it with **Place reference image**, Shift-drag to scale, and use **Fit reference to canvas** to reset. Guide opacity changes the preview. **Include reference in output** composites the guide into PNG and SVG. Use two-finger scroll or middle-drag to pan, pinch or **Alt+scroll** to zoom, and **Home** to frame the image.

Use the mode selector at the upper left of the viewport to enter Object Mode, Edit Mode, Sculpt Mode or Paint Mode. **Tab** switches between Object Mode and Edit Mode. **Shift+Q** opens the mode pie while the pointer is over the viewport. You can also search **Switch interaction mode** with **F3** or open **View → Interaction Mode…**. Object mode is for selecting and moving objects. Edit mode adds **Surface path** and geometry tools. **F3** also exposes the available Box Select, Circle Select, Knife, Fill and Extrude commands. **Measure** and light masks are available from the Object and Edit shelves. Use **Paint Mode** for brush work. Compact mode uses glyphs with hover labels for tools, actions, and toggles, including the ruler controls. Measurements, units, and editable values stay visible.

- **Measure** places rulers on mesh surfaces or the ground. Turn on **Snap vertices** for nearby corners and **Bind to mesh** to follow mesh transforms. Double-click the first point after placing at least three points to close the ruler. Closed rulers also show area. Enter a known total length and choose **Calibrate units** to set the scene’s measurement scale. Select a saved ruler and re-enter Measure to adjust its points.
- **Surface path** places points on one mesh and samples between them when you save. Add points around holes or silhouettes to keep the path on the receiver. Double-click the first point to close the loop before saving. Saved paths can be edited in **Modeling**.
- In **Surface Paint**, choose **Vector stroke** to draw an editable path. **Surface** placement follows a visible mesh or scan and starts a separate path when you cross a gap or change receivers. **View plane** draws in space through the current orbit center. Sample spacing controls the distance between authored points. Escape cancels the gesture and Undo removes the entire stroke.
- **Gaussian splats** paints native 3D Gaussian samples. Choose **Surface** to attach them to the receiver or **View plane** to paint in space. Size and pen pressure set the sample radius. Repeated gestures share a **Splat paint** batch on the receiver, with separate undo for each gesture. The batch’s Gaussian Splat component provides tint, opacity and point display controls.
- **Texture paint** stamps the base-color image on a UV-mapped mesh. The brush projects across visible faces and clips paint to their UV islands. A mesh without a base-color map gets a 1024 × 1024 canvas. Each painted material slot keeps its own image, including when other objects share the original material. Pen pressure controls the stamp radius.
- **Surface dabs** follow the visible surface under the brush. One stroke can cross several unlocked meshes, point clouds, or Gaussian scans, and undoes as a single action. Mesh dabs are clipped against the receiver. Scan dabs use the visible point depth to place a small view-facing mark. The marks follow the receiver’s object transform. Pen pressure changes brush width. Use **[** and **]** to change brush size.

Use **Enter** to apply a mask or save a ruler or path. **Esc** cancels an active operation first; otherwise it clears the selection while keeping the current tool.
